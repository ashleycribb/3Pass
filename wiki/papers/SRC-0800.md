## Abstract & Overview
Tiny Recursive Models (TRM) were recently proposed as a parameter-efficient alternative to large language models (LLMs) for solving Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC) style tasks. The original work reports strong performance and suggests that recursive latent updates enable non-trivial reasoning, but it remains unclear how much of this performance stems from architecture, test-time compute, or task-specific priors. In this technical note, we empirically analyze the publicly released ARC Prize verification checkpoint for TRM on ARC-AGI-1. We report four behavioral findings and a small-scale efficiency comparison. First, we show that test-time augmentation and majority-vote ensembling account for a substantial fraction of reported performance: the 1000-sample voting pipeline improves Pass@1 by 10.75 percentage points over single-pass canonical inference.
